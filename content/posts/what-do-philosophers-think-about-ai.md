{{< pullquote author="Gottlob Frege" >}}
Each good mathematician is at least half a philosopher, and each good philosopher is at least half a mathematician.
{{< /pullquote >}}

Recently, I was reading the essay _The Work of Art in the Age of Mechanical Reproduction_ by Walter Benjamin, which is a response to the rise of technologies such as photography, which “mechanically reproduces” art and cause it to lose what he calls “aura”. I can’t help by draw paralells with the current AI crisis people are facing. AI reproduces human artistic and scientific work in a fashion far more radical than photography, and it challenges the uniqueness, “aura” if you will, of human efforts. Suddenly, we find ourselves compelled to rethink the question of: what really makes us unique? What makes our efforts meaningful?
